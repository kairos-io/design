# Tiles

The Kairos mark is a ring of rhombi in three oranges. Laid flat, the same rhombi make a hexagon, and hexagons tile a plane. The tiles are that pattern: the system's one piece of decoration, built from the mark's own geometry and painted with the brand tokens.

They are the hero of the Foundations deck and of the homepages in this repository. The package carries the same geometry, in shape and order, as the `src/lib/tiles.ts` library, the `Tiles` components and static SVG files. The rules below are the deck's rules.

A hexagon split into three rhombi, each in its tone. Always that order, like the logo: the light tone on top, the mid tone on the left, the Kairos orange on the right. Rotating the unit or swapping the tones breaks the family resemblance with the mark, which is the whole point of the pattern.

## On the site pages

The homepages put a field at the right edge of the hero, dense at that edge and fading towards the copy, which stays on the left under 760px wide. `Hero` does this with one prop: `tiles` takes the field's options (`seed`, `radius`, `height`), `palette="hadron"` paints it in the blues, and `tiles={false}` leaves it out. The Hadron card on kairos.io is the same field in a surface, beside the text, never under it. The stage grids mark each cell with a single `Tile` in its tone, in the order of the mark: Build is the light tile, Deploy the mid, Operate the orange.

## The field

<div className="relative h-60 overflow-hidden rounded-base border bg-bg">
  <TileField fade="right" seed={5} radius={38} />
</div>

A field tiles the plane with units and fills each rhombus with a seeded probability. A **bias** sets that probability by position, so the field is dense at one edge and falls away to outlines at the other, and a few tiles always fall away even where it is densest. The outlines fade with it: each hairline is as opaque as its cell's weight relative to the field's densest cell, so the grid thins out at the same rate as the fills and the sparse edge dissolves into the surface instead of stopping in a line. A cell with a filled face keeps a full outline underneath, so an animated cube always lands on one, and a flat bias draws the complete grid. Three knobs:

## Where it goes

Dense at one edge, fading through outlines to nothing. Hero and empty states only, never behind text. Let the field's sparse edge sit inside the container: it dissolves on its own, so it needs no mask or gradient over it.

## The tile bar

<div className="flex flex-col gap-3">
  <TileBar total={12} done={8} aria-label="Rollout" />
  <TileBar total={5} done={2} size={22} aria-label="Install steps" />
</div>

One parallelogram per node or step. Used for rollouts, install progress and stage markers. Filled tiles cycle through the three tones from the left; the rest are outlines. It is a `progressbar`, so give it a name and the count is announced. When `done` changes, the newly filled tiles transition their colour rather than snapping, so a rollout reads as movement.

## Motion

<div className="relative h-50 overflow-hidden rounded-base border bg-bg">
  <TileField fade="right" seed={5} radius={38} animate="in" />
</div>

A field can arrive instead of being there. `animate="in"` drops the filled tiles into place once, dense edge first, over about a second; each tile's delay comes from its bias weight plus a little jitter (`tileEnterDelay` in `src/lib/tiles.ts`), so the field builds from where it is densest. `animate="live"` is the same entrance, after which a few tiles keep breathing on long uneven cycles of 6 to 11 seconds. Both are CSS only, the `.tile-in` and `.tile-live` classes with their keyframes in `base.css`, so they ship in `styles.css` and need no JavaScript after the first render. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing moves: the field is simply there.

- Use `"in"` on a hero or an empty state that appears once.
- Use `"live"` only on a screen a person leaves open: a dashboard header, a waiting state.
- Never two live fields on one page, and never on content that reflows, because the tiles are positioned by the SVG and a reflow would restart them.

The stories [Tiles into place](?path=/story/components-tiles--animated) and [Live field](?path=/story/components-tiles--live) under Components/Tiles show both.

`animate="cursor"` is a proposal on top of the deck, not part of it, and nothing turns it on by default. The tiles enter as with `"in"`, then follow the pointer over the field's parent (or a `target` element, which `Hero` sets to the whole hero): the cubes around it light up, an empty cell with a faint fill of its tone and a filled cube a shade brighter, falling off with distance and settling back over the slow duration once the pointer leaves. It is opacity only, on a glow layer that shares the grid's points and rests at zero, so the resting field is the static one, no face scales, and a lit cube is still exactly its outline. The field itself stays `pointer-events-none`; the parent is listened to, one animation frame per move, with no React re-render. It only runs on a device that can hover with a fine pointer: touch gets the static field, and so does `prefers-reduced-motion`. See [Follows the cursor](?path=/story/components-tiles--cursor).

## Without a build step

The fields ship as files under `assets/patterns/`: `tile-field-right.svg`, `tile-field-left.svg`, `tile-field-top-right.svg`, `tile-field-wide.svg` and `tile-unit.svg`, and the same five with a `hadron-` prefix (`hadron-tile-field-right.svg` and so on) in the blues. Use one as a background image:

```css
.hero {
  background: url("node_modules/@kairos-io/design/dist/assets/patterns/tile-field-right.svg") right center /
    cover no-repeat;
}
```

The files paint with the `--brand-*` custom properties (`--brand-hadron-*` for the blue set) where `tokens.css` is loaded and fall back to the hex values on their own, so they look the same with or without the rest of the system. Regenerate them with `node scripts/build-patterns.ts` after changing the geometry.
